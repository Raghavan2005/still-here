STILL HERE - design note (Day 1 after the Snap)

1. WHAT I FOCUSED ON, AND WHY
Only the first loop: report -> someone goes -> they record what is needed -> someone is named as responsible.
I chose the moment of the reveal as the core problem: the same address that saves a baby also invites a thief.
So the whole product is built around who sees what, and when. Everything else is kept out.

2. MAIN USER
The responder, a neighbour like Ravi: shaken, walking, one hand free, with a few spare hours.
The relative in Delhi and the nurse use the same 4-step report form. But if the responder
does not turn up, nothing else matters. So the responder's screens (Nearby, Visit, Handover) get most of the care.
The person in need never touches the app. Every screen is written about them, not to them.

3. DECISIONS (and what I rejected)
a) Staged address reveal, instead of a map of pins.
   A map is the obvious design and the one that helps kidnappers most. Instead the list shows only
   "small child, no food or water, ~400 m away". Street appears after "I'll go". Flat and door appear
   only after "I'm outside the building". One open visit per helper, held 90 minutes, every reveal logged
   with the helper's name, and helpers are vouched for by a local group. A thief can still lie their way
   in, which is why a child case also asks the helper to bring a second person. This cuts the risk; it does not remove it.
   The report screen shows the reporter exactly what strangers will and won't see before they send.
   Photos follow the same rule: reporter-supplied, shrunk to ~360 px, hidden in the list, shown only at the
   door after the helper has committed, switched off on weak signal. A photo is what lets a helper find the right person in a
   building, and it is also the most sensitive thing in the app, so it is the last thing released.
b) Trust through consequences, not through blocking.
   I considered requiring verification before a report counts. Rejected: a delay costs lives, and the nurse's
   list is the most reliable data there is. Instead: the reporter says how they know; multiple reports and staff
   reports are shown as such; the helper can close "nobody here, no blame" in one tap; an empty address
   lowers the reporter's reliability; a locked door with no answer stays on the list marked "needs two".
   A report for a baby is never hidden because it is unconfirmed. Wrongly ignoring one costs more than a wasted walk.
c) A visit cannot be closed without a name and a time.
   I considered a free "notes" field. Rejected: notes get written and no one reads them. "Done" stays greyed
   out until someone is named as responsible and a next check is set. If nobody is named, the case returns to
   the list with what the helper found, rather than silently ending. Missed checks make it "overdue".
d) Built for no signal.
   The app is plain files with no downloads. Everything the user taps is saved on the phone and sent when signal
   returns. With no data at all, the banner offers a pre-written SMS. Photos are off on weak signal.
   Urgency is a visible sum (who + what is wrong + hours since care), colour plus shape plus words, so a
   helper can disagree with it and see why.

4. HOW WE WOULD KNOW IT WORKS
- Time from report to a person at the door, by urgency band (target: under 60 minutes for "go now").
- Share of cases with a named responder and a next-check time when the visit closes (target: 100%, because the app enforces it).
- Share of next checks actually done on time. This is the number that shows whether people were abandoned on Day 2.
- "Nobody here" rate per reporter, and any report where an address was passed on but no visit happened (misuse flag).
- Cases still sitting unclaimed after 3 hours: the real shortfall in volunteers.
Not counted: downloads, time in app, reports submitted.

Prototype: open index.html in a browser. Tap the signal chip (top right) to try weak or no network.
